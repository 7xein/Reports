# KPI audit — 12 November 2026

Commissioned because the CEO intends to pay incentives and issue fines from the KPI dashboard. Four independent audits of `lib/kpi.ts` (1,955 lines) and its surrounding code, each from a different angle: advisor-KPI fidelity, workshop-KPI fidelity, unfair-failure paths, and attribution.

**Bottom line: the dashboard is not yet a defensible basis for fines, and not for per-person scoring of any kind.** The arithmetic is sound. The problem is upstream of the arithmetic — several KPIs do not measure what their name says, several turn missing data into a non-compliance, the per-person numbers do not reach the people, and every caveat the engine writes is discarded before it reaches the screen.

## Scope and limits of this audit

This was a **static code audit**. There were no Odoo credentials on the machine it ran on, so nothing was verified against live data. Findings marked *needs-live-data* are certain as code paths, but their frequency in practice depends on how EVS actually uses Odoo. No application code was changed.

Four separate auditors converged on the same defects from different starting points, and the load-bearing claims were independently re-read and confirmed against the source. Where an auditor's claim was wrong, it is marked refuted below rather than quietly dropped.

## 1. What is correct

Worth stating first, because it is substantial and it is what the fixes build on.

- **Nothing-to-measure is handled honestly.** `pct = applicable > 0 ? … : null`, and a null percentage is excluded from every average rather than counted as zero. Nobody is scored down for a week in which a KPI did not apply to them.
- **Percentages cap at 100**, so a KPI cannot pay out above target.
- **No comparison in the engine can be NaN or negative.** `odooTime` returns `null` rather than `0` or epoch, every caller checks it, and `businessMsBetween` clamps to at least 0. The clock-skew class of bug does not exist here. It has been converted into a free pass instead — see section 4.
- **KPI 14 (Check-in after RO) is correct end to end** — right population, the *first* arrival in Inspection as the end event, the right threshold, the business clock, and it refuses to fail a job with no stage history at all.
- **KPI 9 (Weekly closure rate) is correct**, and its exclusion from the achievement average is consistent in all three places a score is formed: the overall, the per-section totals, and the stored weekly history. The page also carries a visible "not in overall" badge.
- **Historical exclusion is near-complete.** Every KPI population excludes `is_historical` repair orders except KPI 19 (section 5).
- **The public-holiday mechanism is fully built and plumbed.** Only the data is missing (section 6).

### The "two 48s" mystery is solved, and it is harmless

A long-standing puzzle: KPIs 2 and 11 both read 48. Those were never counts. `quoteApprovalHours` and `deliveryHours` both default to 48, and `kpiDisplayName` bakes the threshold into the on-screen name, so the page reads "Quote approval ≤ 48 business hrs" and "Delivered within 48 business hrs". The two KPIs share no population, no threshold key and no judging function. Closed, no action.

The genuinely coupled pair is **1 and 11**, not 2 and 11: identical population, identical measurement, one judging function, only the threshold differs. So every late invoice is counted as two separate failures in the achievement average.

## 2. The one that makes everything else indefensible

**The engine's entire disclosure layer is dead code.** `computeKpiTree` returns a `notes` array, and the KPI page has no consumer for it — a search for `notes` in `app/kpi/KpiClient.tsx` and `app/kpi/page.tsx` returns nothing at all.

Separately, the ⓘ tooltip renders only when `cell.snapshot` is true, which is only KPIs 6 and 7. The carefully written explanations for the other nineteen KPIs are computed, attached to the cell, and never shown. The authoritative `rule` strings appear only as hover text on the Admin page.

So all of this is computed and thrown on the floor:

- "KPIs 6 and 7 reflect the current state of open ROs, so they are the same regardless of the week selected."
- "Stage tracking has only N days of history — Inspection and QA will read high."
- "QA duration needs the QA state mapped." KPI 16 is **unmapped by default**, so this fires on a fresh install and the CEO sees a blank row with no reason given.
- "Parts placed skipped N confirmed purchase orders."
- Every "this KPI was skipped, the field is missing" line.

**A figure whose caveats cannot reach the person acting on it is not a defensible basis for a fine.** A fined advisor opening the page sees a name and a percentage, and no statement of method whatsoever.

This is also the cheapest significant fix in the list, and it should come first: it costs no change to any calculation, and it makes every remaining defect visible instead of silent.

## 3. Fails unsafe — missing data becomes a fine

The engine uses a tri-state pattern throughout: judged / failed / still running. In five places the middle branch reads "no data" as "overdue". Each converts a single Odoo hiccup — a lost access right, a renamed field, one network blip — into a near-total non-compliance sweep, with no note on the page to explain the zero. **No code change is needed for these to fire; they are one bad RPC away.**

| KPIs | Mechanism | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 11 | A swallowed `catch` in `invoicePostedTimes` empties the invoice map | Every RO repaired over 1 business hour ago reads non-compliant. Both KPIs read about 0% on a clean-looking page |
| 3 | A swallowed `catch` empties `soApproved` | Every RO with a quotation reads "never approved" |
| 4 | The same failure, opposite direction | Every RO with any quotation reads compliant — **100%, undeserved** |
| 12, 15 | A swallowed `catch` empties `soCreated` | Every RO in Inspection past the limit reads non-compliant |

The one diagnostic note that does fire in this area actively misdescribes the failure: it says the quotation KPIs "only check that a quotation exists rather than that it came first", which is true of KPI 4 and false of KPI 3, which hard-fails.

### Fairness guards that evaporate

Three places where a guard exists, is documented in its own comment as necessary, and silently disappears when the field it depends on is absent or null:

- **KPI 13 (Customer follow-up).** The code only judges QA-flagged ROs, because Odoo keeps the screenshot field read-only on the rest — the comment says judging those "would mark advisors down for a box Odoo never let them fill." If `repair_is_qa` does not exist on the instance, the filter evaporates and every repaired RO is judged for a box it could not fill. The tooltip still claims only QA-flagged ROs are counted.
- **KPI 13 has no grace period at all**, and the config field built for one — `followUpGraceDays`, declared and documented in `lib/types.ts:364` — is read **nowhere in the codebase**. An RO repaired ten minutes before the page loads is already a named violation against the advisor.
- **KPIs 6 and 7 age from `create_date`** when `status_since` is empty on a record. The code's own comment explains why that is wrong: "create_date is only the whole job card's age and overstates every wait." A job opened 100 days ago that began waiting for labour yesterday is aged at 100 days against a 2-day limit. Every step of that fallback chain moves toward *more* elapsed time, so every ambiguity resolves as a fine.

### One keystroke fines everyone

In Admin → KPI configuration, clearing a threshold box saves **`0`** (`parseInt('') || 0`). Every engine read is `config.thresholds.X ?? default`, and `0` is not nullish, so the default never applies: the limit becomes zero and nothing can be inside it. There is no validation, no confirmation, and no note. This affects roughly fourteen KPIs from a single empty field.

## 4. Measurements that do not match their names

### Undeserved compliance — an incentive paid for nothing

- **KPI 5 (Tag on creation) never looks at when the tag was added** — only whether one exists now. A tag added three days late scores as on-time. The KPI as written cannot measure its own rule.
- **KPIs 1 and 11 take the *earliest* invoice** on the sale order. Any Odoo deposit or advance invoice posted at quotation time predates the repair, and a negative interval clamps to zero, so the job is compliant forever regardless of when the real closing invoice posted.
- **The `invoice_date` fallback is day-resolution**, parsed at midnight UTC — before the working day starts. An invoice posted days late but dated the repair day lands *before* the repair and passes unconditionally.
- **KPIs 3, 4, 12, 15 and 16 are laundered by ordinary rework.** They use the *most recent* transition into a state, so a job that bounces back to Inspection or Under Repair is measured from the later entry. A genuinely unquoted re-inspection can never fail: the quotation predates the latest Inspection entry, the interval goes negative, and negative clamps to compliant. KPI 14 gets this right by taking the *first* transition — there is a correct house pattern already in the file to copy.
- **KPI 7 reads a clean 100% if its priority code drifts.** The query returns zero rows whether nothing is stuck or the mapping matches nothing in Odoo, and the code cannot tell those apart. Everything is then marked compliant with no note. KPI 6's state fallback has the same shape.
- **KPI 20 counts an unreadable parts line as a received part** — `!lineInfo[id]?.short` is `true` when the entry is missing. One timed-out chunk read declares whole jobs complete.

### Unfair non-compliance — a fine for something not done wrong

- **KPI 22 (Ready by promised time) takes the *last* of three "Repaired" codes** (Labour Complete / QC Complete / Vehicle Ready). A car finished at 16:00 on the promised day but marked Vehicle Ready the next morning is scored late against a promise it met.
- **KPI 22 does not end-of-day a datetime promise.** The 23:59:59 treatment only applies if the field's type is `date`; on Odoo 17 `schedule_date` is a Datetime, so a date-only entry stores 00:00 and a car ready at any time that day fails. *Needs-live-data on the field type.*
- **KPI 2's population is unbounded in time.** The open-quotation query filters on state and company only — no date window at all, no limit. Every draft quotation ever written is re-scored every week, forever: a two-year-old abandoned quote from a departed advisor fails this week, next week, and every week until someone confirms it. Selecting a past week still judges today's drafts against today's clock, so last month's report mutates as the backlog grows.
- **KPI 16 can score a different week's QA dwell than the one on screen.** The population is window-scoped but the timeline scan is not, so it takes the last QA arrival in all of history. A week-45 overrun is unreachable while a week-47 dwell is scored into week 45.
- **KPI 18 credits one job's RFQ to another job on the same vehicle.** Any purchase order on that vehicle inside the window counts for every open job card on it. The EVHUB report explicitly documents this collision as real on this data.
- **KPI 20 can judge a purchase order against a job card that was already closed** — `jobFor` requires non-historical, not open.
- **KPI 19 fines the buyer for a manager's approval delay.** `to approve` is among the counted states, and the non-compliance is charged to the order's creator, who cannot clear it.

## 5. The parts chain does not measure one thing

KPIs 18, 19 and 20 sit under one heading and disagree about the population, the unit and the week:

- **Unit.** KPI 18 counts repair orders; 19 and 20 count purchase orders. One job with five RFQs is 1 record in 18 and 5 in 19.
- **Anchor.** Three different events — quotation approval, order creation, order confirmation — slice three different weeks. An RFQ raised Thursday of week 45 and confirmed Saturday of week 46 appears in 19's week-45 row and 20's week-46 row. The chain cannot reconcile inside one week by construction.
- **Scope.** KPI 19's population is every purchase order created in the week, with no vehicle requirement, no job-card requirement, and **no historical exclusion** — because `notHistorical()` is a `repair.order` clause and `purchase.order` carries no link back to a repair order. So KPI 19 scores purchases for pre-clean-slate job cards, for vehicles with no open job, and for shop consumables, tooling and stationery.
- **Company.** KPIs 19 and 20 are scoped to tracked companies and then dropped for any company outside `WORKSHOP_BRANCHES`, which excludes EV Hub. **KPI 18's purchase-order query has no company filter at all.** If workshop parts are bought through EV Hub, 18 reads high while 19 and 20 read near-empty.

**Worked example.** A Dubai storeman raises eleven purchase orders in week 45 — three for live jobs, four against vehicles whose job cards were moved to Historical, four for consumables with no vehicle. Seven sit unconfirmed past the limit. KPI 19 reports 4/11 = **36%** against a 90% target. KPI 18 reports 100%. KPI 20 never sees eight of the eleven. The branch is fined on 36% for purchasing that neither neighbouring KPI regards as in scope — and because a consumables order's creator is often not on the roster, the failures land at branch level with no drill-down.

## 6. Public holidays

**Correction to an earlier assumption: the mechanism is fully built.** `businessMsBetween` takes a holiday list, honours it on partial end days and in both the short-span and long-span paths, `computeKpiTree` passes `config.publicHolidays ?? []` through the single `elapsed` closure that every business-clock comparison in the engine uses, and Admin has a field for entering them. Nothing needs building. **Only the data is missing.**

With none entered, a public holiday is treated as an ordinary working day and burns about 9 business hours of limit per day. At the shipped defaults, for a three-day Eid:

| KPI | Limit | Consumed | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Invoice on delivery | 1 bh | 27 bh | 27× limit — guaranteed fail |
| 17 Chatter response | 1 bh | 27 bh | 27× — guaranteed fail |
| 14 Check-in | 3 bh | 27 bh | 9× — guaranteed fail |
| 12 Quote sent | 4 bh | 27 bh | 6.75× — guaranteed fail |
| 16 QA duration | 9 bh | 27 bh | 3× — guaranteed fail |
| 15 Inspection | 27 bh | 27 bh | exactly 100% of the limit |
| 2, 11 | 48 bh | 27 bh | 56% of the limit consumed |

A **single one-day** national holiday already exceeds the limit outright for KPIs 1, 14 and 17. The affected KPIs are exactly the business-clock ones: 1, 2, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17.

**And a trap.** The Admin field accepts any text and stores it unvalidated. The clock matches only zero-padded `YYYY-MM-DD`; anything else — `2026-12-2`, `02/12/2026`, `Dec 2 2026` — is saved, shown back to the admin exactly as typed, and silently excluded from nothing, while the page continues to promise that holidays are excluded. Entering the holidays could *appear* to work and not. The validation must land together with the data.

## 7. Attribution — why per-person scoring does not hold

- **Eight KPIs reach nobody's individual card.** KPIs 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 12, 15 and 16 are charged to the repair order's creator — the service advisor — but all eight are `section: 'workshop'`, and a person is scored only on their own section's KPIs. So an advisor's card omits them, and a workshop-rostered technician has no evaluations at all because he created no repair orders. The violations count at branch and company level and land on no individual. Both halves are deliberate and separately commented; together they cancel out. This is made systematic by the roster suggester, which proposes `advisor` for anyone who creates repair orders.
- **Branch and person are computed from different populations.** The branch cell is every evaluation booked to that company, roster or not, both sections. A person's cell is all of their work in any branch, narrowed to their section. This is deliberate and documented locally — but it means an advisor's percentage and their branch's percentage are drawn from different sets and can differ by the entire population of a KPI. The branch figure is the authoritative one. The file header promises the opposite: "SA / branch / company numbers all come from the same source and can never disagree." That promise is now false, which is the most dangerous property here — the numbers look reconciled, so nobody will check them.
- **Off-roster work counts at branch level and on nobody's card**, with no warning. An integration user, OdooBot, an admin or a new hire generates violations that no named person carries.
- **Cross-branch work splits.** The branch comes from the record's company; the person is placed by their roster branch. A Dubai advisor who misses a mention on a Sharjah job fails Sharjah's KPI while appearing only on Dubai's list.
- **KPI 17 can fine a branch for a customer not replying.** The candidates to answer a chatter message are taken raw from the message's recipient partners, with nothing restricting them to internal users. Odoo's *Send message* puts the customer's partner on that list. The customer never comments in the chatter, so after one business hour the branch takes a non-compliance. The partner-to-user mapping is resolved *after* judgement and used only for the display name, so the information needed to prevent this is fetched and then not used. Because the resulting user id is null, the fine appears on no advisor's page and cannot be audited from the dashboard. *Needs-live-data: depends on whether advisors use Send message or Log note.*
- **A mid-week branch move** retroactively relocates the person's whole week onto the new branch's list, while the branch cells keep the work where it was booked.
- **A roster entry whose branch is not a workshop branch** (`EV Hub`, a misspelling) silently gets no card anywhere while its work still scores a branch.
- **An advisor's "Workshop" section score is a single chatter KPI.** KPI 17 is in `KPI_IDS_FOR_EVERYONE` and defaults to the workshop section, so one missed mention renders "WORKSHOP 0%" on an advisor's card under a full section header.

**Refuted.** A suspected inconsistency between `cell.section` and `sectionTotals` is not a live bug: `sectionById` is precomputed over every KPI with the admin override applied, so the fallback never fires and the two agree. It remains latent fragility — any future call site omitting the argument would place a row under one section while averaging it into the other.

## 8. Snapshot KPIs corrupt the stored history

KPIs 6 and 7 ignore the week selector entirely — their population is bounded by `now − 90 days` and their overdue set by `now − limit`. Three consequences:

1. **Opening week 45 today stamps today's backlog into week 45's permanent record.** The API writes `kpiHistory[weekStart] = summariseTree(tree)`, and `summariseTree` copies every cell's percentage including the snapshot ones. The trend line for 6 and 7 is therefore a log of *when someone happened to open each week*, not a trend — and it is rendered in the same delta component as every real week-over-week change.
2. **Double jeopardy.** A vehicle stuck 100 days is a failure in weeks 44, 45, 46 and 47 alike. The same car fines the same branch every week until it moves.
3. **The UI does not make it unmissable.** A hover-only ⓘ, no visible text, the week selector directly above the figure, and the page captioned "Saturday → Friday". On a screenshot, a print-out or a phone, nothing tells the reader the number is not that week's.

Separately, **their denominator is not what their rule says.** The rule for KPI 6 describes repair orders *with outstanding parts*; the code scores *every open repair order* in the 90-day baseline, compliant unless stuck. With 500 open ROs, 60 short of parts and 20 of those overdue, the page shows **96%** where the rule as printed reads **67%** — roughly 30 points kinder than its own stated rule, against a 90% target. The tooltip is honest about this; the `rule` string is not, and the tooltip is one of the only two that actually render.

**These two cannot be used in weekly scoring.** They are a live backlog board.

## 8a. Live findings from the Admin field report

Added after the audit, from the Admin diagnostic read against the live Odoo
instance. These settle several *needs-live-data* findings above and surface two
problems larger than any of them. Both are **configuration, not code**.

### Dubai's main branch contributes zero to everything

The diagnostic reports `"EVS ELECTRIC VEHICLE SERVICE DXB" (Dubai) — CONFIGURED
BUT NOT FOUND IN ODOO`, and that company shows `none` for invoices. Meanwhile an
uncounted company named **`EVS Dubai`** carries **529 posted invoices,
AED 3,519,316** in thirty days.

`BRANCH_CLUSTERS` (`lib/odoo.ts:29`) and `SUB_BRANCH_COMPANY`
(`lib/types.ts:29`) both still name the old company. So "Dubai" on every page of
this dashboard means **Emarat - Altwar plus Emarat - Albarsha only** — the main
workshop is invisible to the WIP report, the sales report, every KPI, and the
CEO's money-at-risk figure. This is almost certainly a company rename in Odoo
that the dashboard never followed.

Also uncounted: `EVS Oman` (81 posted, AED 10,802), `Enoc - Abu Dhabi` (225
posted, AED 0), `CarDIP Dubai`, `CarDIP Abu Dhabi`, four more Enoc companies,
and three junk entries named `1`, `=` and `]`. Some are presumably deliberate;
they should be confirmed one way or the other rather than left ambiguous.

### The "Repaired" moment is read from a field with no history

`Chatter changes tracked in the last 30 days: state=25639, repair_state=46416,
priority_matrix_status=0.`

The default stage map points `repaired` at the Priority Matrix
(`{ kind: 'priority', values: ['C','G','D'] }`), and `trackedField` resolves that
kind to `priority_matrix_status`. That field records **zero** tracked changes, so
no transition times can be recovered from it at all.

Consequence: **KPIs 1, 11, 13 and 22 cannot see when a vehicle became Repaired.**
They fall back to "created this week and currently repaired" — a different and
narrower population than any of their rules describe. The engine does push a note
about the fallback; until stage 0 shipped, nothing rendered it.

**The fix is one change in Admin**, not in code: point `repaired` at Repair
Status (`repair_state`), whose options include `Repaired` and which carries
46,416 tracked changes. That also resolves the KPI 22 "last of three Repaired
codes" defect in section 4 for free — with a single value there is only one
transition to find.

Two related notes. `closed`, `awaitingParts` and `awaitingLabour` also sit on the
Priority Matrix, but they are used as *current-value* filters rather than
transition lookups, so they still work. And `qa` ships as
`{ kind: 'repairState', values: [] }` — **empty**, so **KPI 16 (QA duration) is
unmapped and scores nothing**, which is the blank row the discarded note was
explaining. Repair Status offers both `QA` and `After QA`; picking one in Admin
switches the KPI on.

### `repair_is_qa` may not mean what KPI 13 assumes

The field exists, so the audit's fear that KPI 13's guard would evaporate does
not fire. But its label is **"QA Access"**, and it sits in a family of identically
named booleans: `repair_is_inspection` (Inspection Access), `repair_is_confirm`,
`repair_is_checkin`, `repair_is_start_repair`, `repair_is_end`, `repair_go_back`
— all "Access". That pattern is characteristic of *computed button-visibility*
fields: whether the reading user may press QA, not whether this repair order was
ever QA'd.

If that is what it is, KPI 13's population filter is governed by the integration
user's permissions rather than by the record, and would pass either everything or
nothing. For scale: 824 repair orders carry a follow-up screenshot, all time, all
companies. **This needs checking in Odoo before KPI 13 is used for anything.**

### What the live data confirms as safe

- `is_parts_available` exists, so KPI 18 is active rather than skipped.
- `status_since` exists and `stage_id` / `date_last_stage_update` do not, so
  `resolveAgeField` settles on `status_since` — the correct choice for ageing
  KPIs 6 and 7.
- `is_historical` exists, with `historical_date` and `historical_user_id`.
- `under_warranty` exists on `repair.order`, which is the boolean the CEO
  report's warranty exclusion detects.
- `purchase.order` carries `fleet_id`, `date_approve` (Confirmation Date) and
  `effective_date` (Arrival), so KPIs 18, 19 and 20 all have the fields they need.

### Two figures that size earlier findings

- **139 selectable users, 32 of whom created a repair order in the last 90
  days.** The roster should hold roughly 32 people; every active creator missing
  from it is work scoring a branch and nobody (section 7).
- **1,566 purchase orders created in the last thirty days**, against a workshop
  of this size. KPI 19 scores all of them, including consumables and orders
  against historical job cards (section 5). That problem is material, not
  theoretical.

Also confirmed: `purchase.order` state values include `to approve`, so KPI 19
does charge the buyer for a manager's approval delay.

## 9. Decisions for the business

These are not code questions and should not be settled by whoever writes the fix.

1. **KPI 6/7 denominator** — all open repair orders (today's behaviour, reads about 96%) or only those actually waiting (reads about 67%)? This moves the number by roughly 30 points and changes whether it reads as an incentive or a fine.
2. **Should KPIs 6 and 7 be scored at all**, given section 8? Recommendation: mark them "reference only, not scored" until their population is redefined.
3. **KPI 19 scope.** Recommended: require a vehicle and a live job card, adopting KPI 20's predicate but evaluated at creation time; report what that removes in a note; and add the missing company filter to KPI 18 so all three agree on which companies count.
4. **KPI 2's time bound** — how old may an open quotation be and still be scored?
5. **Who owns the eight workshop KPIs** — reassign roster sections, or re-attribute to a named controller per branch the way KPI 18 already does for its RFQ coordinator?
6. **The public holiday dates** for UAE and Qatar covering the incentive period.
7. **Evidence above 50 violations.** The count is complete but the listed references stop at 50, flagged as "50+". Someone disputing a fine with more than 50 cases cannot see them all.

## 10. Recommended order of work

**Stage 0 — make the engine tell the truth on screen.** Render the `notes` array on the KPI page; render the ⓘ for every KPI, not only the snapshots; correct the misfiled tooltips (19 shows KPI 21's rule, 20 shows KPI 22's, 21 and 22 have none, 18 describes a measurement the code does not perform and the wrong clock); add the missing notes for KPIs 5 and 8. No calculation changes. Nothing else on this list is defensible without it, and it is the cheapest item here.

**Stage 1 — stop the wrong fines.** The five swallowed catches in section 3 must exclude the record and push a note instead of scoring a failure. Honour `followUpGraceDays` and restore KPI 13's QA guard. Reject `0` thresholds. Stop KPIs 6 and 7 ageing from `create_date`. Restrict KPI 17's candidates to internal users. Validate the holiday input and warn when the list is empty.

**Stage 2 — fix the measurements.** First transition instead of last for KPIs 3, 4, 12, 15, 16 and 22, copying KPI 14's pattern. Stop a deposit invoice satisfying KPIs 1 and 11, and stop accepting a midnight `invoice_date` as a posting time. End-of-day a datetime promise on KPI 22. Decide whether KPI 5 can measure its own rule at all — if tag additions are not tracked in the chatter, it cannot, and it should say so rather than report a number.

**Stage 3 — the decisions in section 9**, then the attribution model.

## In the meantime

If the CEO wants to begin this week, the asymmetry matters: a wrong incentive costs money, while a wrong fine costs trust and is a genuine injustice to a named person. **Start with recognition at branch level on a named subset of KPIs, and hold fines until at least Stage 1 is done.** KPI 14 is sound today. KPI 9 is sound but structurally depressed by design and excluded from the achievement average. Of the rest, none is currently safe to fine from.
